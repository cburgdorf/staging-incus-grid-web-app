Map layers

Sailing Routes

Connections between harbours and anchorages that cruising boats actually sailed, for the whole year or a single month. They show when a crossing makes sense, in which direction, and where crews set off and arrive.

Thicker lines mean more popular connections in the selected period; arrows show the direction. Zooming in reveals shorter connections. The popularity slider hides less popular connections. Select a line to see in which months it is sailed, separately for each direction.

Boat Stays

Areas where boats paused their position reports and later reported again nearby: typical places to leave a boat for a few days, a season or the winter.

Choose a pause length, from a few days to more than six months. Areas where boats pause more often stand out more. Select an area for its median pause and the months the pauses overlap. Boat stays are derived from reporting pauses, not from confirmed berths.

Hotspots

Where boats spend their time in each month: how long boats sailed or stayed in an area of about 15 nautical miles, from their position reports and stays. Colours and the hotspot index (0–100) are relative, from little to much activity in our data; they do not count all yachts.

Choose a month to watch the season move, for example to the Caribbean in winter and the Mediterranean in summer. All months share one scale; the year shows an average month. Select an area to compare its months.

Wind

How often a wind strength occurs, and from which direction, for each month or the whole year.

Colours show the share of hours with the selected strength: light 6–10 kn, moderate 10–18 kn, strong 18–30 kn. With full access, tap the map to see all twelve months at one point and the prevailing direction.

Waves

How often calm, moderate, rough or very rough seas occur, by month or over the year.

Colours show the share of hours in the selected sea state. The classes combine wave height and steepness, so short, steep waves count as rougher than a long swell of the same height. With full access, tap the map to compare the months at one point.

Thunderstorm Days

How often thunderstorms occurred: the share of days on which the WWLLN lightning network detected lightning in the same area of about 50 × 50 km, 2013–2025.

Each colour stands for the same share of days in every month, so months and places can be compared. 10 % means about 3 days in a month. The grid is coarse: it describes the sea area, not a single bay.

Read more on the blog →

Lightning Density

How much lightning there was: the average number of strokes per 100 km² and month that the WWLLN lightning network detected in an area of about 50 × 50 km, 2013–2025, adjusted for the network's detection rate in each region. The year view shows the average month.

Thunderstorm days show how often storms occur, lightning density how intense they are. An area with few thunderstorm days but high density has rare but strong storms. Every month uses the same scale. The network misses some strokes, so the numbers are best used for comparing places and months.

Thunderstorm Potential

How often the atmosphere offered conditions that favour thunderstorms, by month or over the year, including places without recorded strikes.

Each colour stands for the same share of time in every month, based on ERA5 weather data from 1994–2023. Where the potential is high but thunderstorm days are rare, conditions often allowed storms that then did not form.

Terrain Exposure

How well the surrounding terrain may shield a spot on the water from lightning. For every location the app looks for the highest terrain within about 350 m and how far it rises above the water.

Purple means little shielding (exposed), sand medium, green strong shielding from nearby hills and cliffs. Open water without terrain nearby stays uncoloured. Compare the sides of a bay: the greener side is better shielded. The layer appears from zoom level 7.

Frequently asked questions

Is IncusGrid a forecast or a navigation aid?

No. IncusGrid shows historical patterns for planning. It is not a weather forecast and not a navigation aid. Always check current forecasts, official charts and local conditions before you go to sea.

Where does the data come from?

Sailing routes, boat stays and hotspots come from thousands of real boat movements, aggregated by area so that individual boats are not shown. Wind and waves come from many years of global weather and wave model data, thunderstorm potential from ERA5, thunderstorm days and lightning density from the WWLLN lightning network, and terrain exposure from detailed 30 m elevation data.

Why does the route list change when I move the popularity slider?

Connections are bundled by region. When none of the detailed connections inside a corridor is popular enough for the slider setting, the app keeps the bundled corridor (for example Ionian Islands → Sicily), which combines them. Raising the slider can therefore move such a corridor to the top.

Why do route lines cross land?

Lines connect the start and end of observed journeys in simplified form. They show which connections are sailed, not the way to sail them.

Why is Terrain Exposure empty when I zoom out?

The analysis uses detailed 30 m elevation data and appears from zoom level 7. Zoom in to a coastal area.

What does “share of hours” mean?

For wind and waves it is the percentage of all hours in the selected period that fell into the chosen class, over all years of data. 20 % in July means about one hour in five.

Access and subscription

What is free?

Without an account you can look at every layer for the whole year and for January and February, open three routes or boat stays and inspect three spots on the map. A free account adds March and April and opens all routes, boat stays and spots, with no time limit and no payment details.

What does full access add?

All twelve months instead of the annual view and January to April, the seasonal details of routes and boat stays for every month, hotspots month by month, both directions of a route, all twelve months at any spot on the map, wind by time of day with storm days, gust days, quiet nights and strong wind duration, boat stays of every length, wave details such as swell, short steep sea and rough days, Terrain Exposure and Thunderstorm Potential.

Which plans are there?

Full access is available monthly (starting with the free first month), yearly, or, for a limited time only, as Lifetime: a one-time payment for permanent full access, without a subscription.

How do I cancel my subscription?

Sign in, open Billing from the account menu and choose “Cancel subscription”. You keep full access until the end of the period you have paid for, and there is no further charge. If you cancel during the free first month, you are not charged at all.

Is there an app?

Yes, IncusGrid is available for Android on Google Play, and it runs in any browser on phone, tablet or laptop. Your account and subscription work both in the app and on the web.

Account and sign-in

How do I sign in?

With Google, with Apple, with a one-time code sent to your email address, or with a password. With Apple you can choose to hide your email address.

How do I delete my account?

Send an email to hello@incusgrid.com from the email address associated with your account so we can identify it. We will delete your account and all associated data and confirm the deletion within a few business days. If you have an active subscription, please cancel it first.

Contact

Questions, feedback or a problem with the app? Write to hello@incusgrid.com. We usually reply within a day.
